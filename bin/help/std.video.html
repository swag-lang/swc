Video

Video reads and writes timed sequences of Pixel.Image frames. File-backed AVI, ISO-BMFF, and Matroska readers also expose streamed sound tracks through Audio.SoundFile, and Matroska readers expose timed text through Video.Subtitle.Track. Standalone subtitle documents use the same track and cue model.

Reading and writing are streamed: Video.Reader opens a stream by reading its metadata and decodes one frame at a time, and Video.Writer encodes one frame at a time into its destination. Encoded media payloads remain in their source; indexed containers retain compact sample tables while decoding keeps only the active picture, codec references, and bounded audio buffers. Video.Clip is the other half of the picture — the frames a program builds in memory before encoding them. Video.Timebase converts absolute timestamps between integer media clocks, including nanosecond capture timestamps and video or audio track ticks, without cumulative rounding.

#import("video") using Video let rate = try FrameRate.create(24, 1) var clip = try Clip.create(320, 180, rate) let frame = Pixel.Image.create(320, 180, .RGB8) try clip.addFrame(frame) try clip.save("preview.y4m") var reader = try Reader.open("preview.y4m") var picture: Pixel.Image for index in reader.frameCount() do try reader.decodeFrameInto(&picture, index)

A format is a codec registered against Video.IDecoder and Video.IEncoder, selected by filename extension; both read and write through Core.ByteSource and Core.ByteSink, which are what make a codec independent of files and memory alike. Four codecs ship in the box:

  • YUV4MPEG2 (.y4m) reads progressive 8-bit monochrome, 4:2:0, 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 YCbCr frames and writes 4:4:4 ones. It does not compress, so it favors a small implementation over the size of what it produces.
  • AVI (.avi) reads Motion JPEG, MPEG-4 Part 2, and uncompressed frames, exposes integer PCM sound tracks, and writes Motion JPEG pictures. A coded stream states its codec through the bitmap header of the container and its setup headers inside the stream, and stores a bidirectional plane behind the plane it refers forward to, which is what this container's lack of a decoding order costs. The container index locates Motion JPEG and uncompressed frames directly. MPEG-4 Part 2 seeks to a preceding sync picture and reconstructs prediction dependencies through the requested frame.
  • ISO-BMFF (.mp4, .m4v, and .mov) reads Motion JPEG, H.264, and H.265 pictures, writes Motion JPEG, and exposes AAC-LC mono/stereo audio. Video.Mp4.FragmentedWriter separately writes already encoded H.264 packets as bounded fragments, optionally interleaved with up to four AAC-LC sound tracks, and can resume after the last complete fragment of a stopped recording. The writer reports submitted video and audio packet counts, track end times, and completed byte count for recording progress and file-size limits. Video.Mp4.FragmentedPacketReader exposes their original compressed packets and track clocks for copying without decoding. Video.Mp4.FragmentedPacketCursor walks the same video and AAC packets one fragment at a time, keeping a bounded sample window; it reports each packet's fragment location and the last complete media boundary, and can start at a key-frame fragment located through Video.Mp4.FragmentedIndex. Video.Mp4.summarizeFragmentedRecording scans with the same bounded memory to report track packet counts, decode-time endpoints, the last complete media boundary, and an interrupted tail. Video.Mp4.copyVideoRanges joins key-frame-aligned H.264 ranges into a video-only file without re-encoding; Video.Mp4.copyRanges also copies AAC-LC tracks, rounding cut edges to whole sound packets. Video.Mp4.planVideoTimeRanges expands intervals on the video decode-time clock to safe key-frame packet ranges for either copy operation. Video.Mp4.FragmentedIndex reads an optional final key-frame index from the file tail and locates a fragment without scanning media data; the cursor can seek directly to its video key frame. Video.Mp4.rebuildFragmentedIndex can finalize complete fragments after an interrupted write and rebuild that index using temporary disk storage. Video.Reader reads samples by scanning fragment headers and recovers complete fragments after an interrupted final write. The classic reader expands compact sample tables into direct packet offsets while payloads remain in the file. Exact composition timestamps are retained for variable-rate playback and coded display reordering. Video.H264.AnnexBAccessUnit converts start-code-delimited H.264 encoder output into the length-prefixed packets and avcC configuration the fragmented MP4 writer consumes. Video.Nv12Frame converts packed RGB images into BT.709 NV12 surfaces for a video encoder. Video.H264.Encoder produces Constrained Baseline packets from those surfaces with flat intra blocks, exact inter copies for small screen movements, luma and chroma residuals, P-skip for unchanged blocks, and PCM fallback. QP zero retains exact output; an optional positive QP quantizes both IDR and P-picture residuals against locally reconstructed neighbours and references. The fixed QP is not rate control. Video.Mp4.SoftwareRecording joins RGB-to-NV12 conversion, software H.264 and AAC encoding, sequential frame slots, zero through four separately encoded sound tracks, arbitrary PCM chunks, AAC priming when audio is present, and fragmented MP4 finalization. Video.FramePacer assigns monotonic capture timestamps to exact constant-rate video slots and reports skipped frames without retaining pictures.
  • Matroska (.mkv) reads H.264, H.265, MPEG-4 Part 2, and RealVideo 4 pictures and exposes every AAC-LC, AC-3, independent E-AC-3, DTS Core, FLAC, MPEG Layer III, Vorbis, and Opus audio track with up to eight channels that its decoder supports. A picture track remuxed from a legacy file is read as well: such a track names its codec through a Video for Windows header, states its setup headers inside the stream rather than in the container, and may store a bidirectional plane behind the plane it refers forward to. Display order is then taken from the clock the stream states, not from the order the container hands samples over. RealVideo 4 is decoded in Swag, including intra, predicted, and bidirectional pictures, multiple slices, and its loop filter. Its wrapping picture clock also repairs legacy container timestamps that put bidirectional pictures after their future reference. Frames stored without the bytes they all begin with, which is what mkvmerge does by default to several codecs, are handed to the decoder whole; a track compressed or encrypted any other way is not offered. A file whose tail never made it to disk is read as far as it goes. Matroska CodecDelay is removed from the audible timeline. Opening reads only seek, track, and cue metadata. Decoding advances one foreground demux cursor through complete cluster windows as playback reaches them; random access restarts it at the preceding video cue and reconstructs the coded prediction through the requested picture. No full-file scan competes with playback. Sound tracks become available when that progressive cursor reaches their playback window; S_TEXT/UTF8, S_TEXT/ASS, and S_TEXT/SSA subtitle tracks are exposed on that same media timeline. The complete indexes continue in the background, and work may be interrupted through Video.Reader.requestCancel. Media payloads stay in the file.

Video.Subtitle.Track.loadOwned decodes SubRip (.srt), WebVTT (.vtt), ASS/SSA (.ass and .ssa), LRC (.lrc), and MicroDVD (.sub) documents. UTF-8, UTF-16, UTF-32, and Windows-1252 text are detected. The decoder keeps cue timing and readable text while leaving font, color, position, outline, and synchronization choices to the player.

Decoding and encoding are separate implementations of a format, under src/decode and src/encode, laid out as the Pixel image codecs are. A format that has something to choose takes an options structure the same way they do, such as Avi.EncodeOptions and Mp4.EncodeOptions.

Unsupported picture or sound codecs fail or remain unavailable rather than being mistaken for decoded media. Alpha is discarded during encoding, and decoded frames use Pixel.PixelFormat.RGB8. Malformed streams, unsupported codecs, inconsistent frames, and file access errors use Swag's normal fail mechanism.

Reading and writing video

A video is read as a stream and written as a stream. Video.Reader opens a file by reading its metadata and decodes one frame at a time; Video.Writer encodes one frame at a time into its destination. Encoded media stays in the source. Readers retain container indexes, codec reference pictures, and bounded decoding and audio buffers; they do not retain every decoded frame. Video.Clip holds the frames a program builds in memory before encoding them.

Playing a stream

var reader = try Video.Reader.open("clip.y4m") var frame: Pixel.Image for index in reader.frameCount() do try reader.decodeFrameInto(&frame, index)

Video.Reader.decodeFrameInto reuses the caller's output image when its geometry and format match. Codec references and parallel decoding keep additional bounded buffers. Reading frames in order avoids restarting prediction from a sync point; random access has the cost of the selected codec and container. Video.Reader.frameCount returns zero when the format cannot report a count without decoding the stream, and a stream read that way ends by failing rather than by returning a frame.

The file stays open until the reader is closed, so it must remain available and unchanged. Video.Reader.openMemory reads an encoded stream already held in memory, borrowing it rather than copying it.

Producing a video

Create an empty clip with fixed geometry and a Video.FrameRate, add matching images, then call Video.Clip.save. Use Video.Writer directly when the frames are produced one at a time and never all exist together, which is what a recorder or a renderer does.

The encoders accept every 8-bit Pixel RGB or BGR format and drop alpha. A decoded round trip differs from what was written by a few channel values, and by more than that on a format that compresses.

Reading sound tracks

File-backed AVI, ISO-BMFF, and Matroska readers expose playable sound tracks through Video.Reader.audioTrackCount and Video.Reader.audioTrack. Each Audio.SoundFile owns a compact packet table and reopens the container through its own cursor, so sound and picture stream in parallel without sharing a seek position or retaining compressed payloads. Matroska preserves every supported track and puts the container's default track at index zero.

Memory-backed readers expose pictures but no sound track because a voice needs an independently owned streaming cursor. AVI exposes integer PCM tracks and ISO-BMFF AAC-LC mono/stereo tracks. Matroska exposes AAC-LC, AC-3, the supported independent E-AC-3 profile, DTS Core, FLAC, Layer III, Vorbis, and Opus tracks within the channel counts their decoders support, preserves their speaker order, and subtracts CodecDelay priming from playback and seeking. Unsupported sound codecs remain unavailable while a supported picture track stays playable.

Controlling how a stream is encoded

A format that has something to configure takes an options structure, exactly as the Pixel image encoders do. Pass it to Video.Clip.save, Video.Clip.encode or Video.Writer.create; passing nothing takes the defaults of the selected format.

try clip.save("capture.avi", Video.Avi.EncodeOptions{quality: 80})

Avi.EncodeOptions and Mp4.EncodeOptions both select the JPEG quality of their Motion JPEG frames. YUV4MPEG2 writes one plane layout and one colour range and so has nothing to choose.

Adding a format

A format is a codec registered against Video.IDecoder and Video.IEncoder and selected by filename extension, exactly like the image codecs of the Pixel module — and laid out the same way, one subfolder per format on each side, even when that subfolder contains one source file:

src/decode/reader.swg the registry, and Video.Reader src/decode/y4m/y4m.swg Y4m.Decoder, and the layout of the format src/decode/avi/avi.swg Avi.Decoder, and the layout of the container src/decode/mp4/mp4.swg Mp4.Decoder, and the ISO-BMFF sample tables src/decode/matroska/ Matroska.Decoder, EBML, tracks, blocks, and lacing src/encode/writer.swg the registry, and Video.Writer src/encode/y4m/y4m.swg Y4m.Encoder src/encode/avi/avi.swg Avi.Encoder src/encode/mp4/mp4.swg Mp4.Encoder

Decoding and encoding one format are two independent implementations that share only its binary layout, and that layout is declared on the decoding side, where reading it comes first.

A codec reads through Core.ByteSource and writes through Core.ByteSink, which are what keep it independent of files and memory alike, and it implements random access itself: a fixed frame size computes an offset, an indexed container reads the index, and a format with inter-coded frames seeks to a keyframe and decodes forward.

A container whose header carries a total it only learns at the end — the size of the file, the number of frames, the offset of an index — reserves that field when it writes the header and rewrites it with Core.ByteSink.patch once the last frame lands. That is what keeps a writer at the memory cost of one frame instead of the cost of the result, and it is how the AVI encoder works.

What a codec is tested against

Decoder conformance uses independent files from src/tests/datas/, alongside focused malformed-input and encoder/decoder roundtrip tests. The external corpus includes a camera recording, standard research sequences, synthetic clips encoded by FFmpeg, and edited container layouts that no single writer produces. datas/THIRDPARTY.md records their sources, coverage, and redistribution terms. Roundtrip tests complement that corpus; they cannot establish interoperability on their own.

struct H264.Encoder #src

Encodes NV12 pictures as H.264 access units for a fragmented MP4 writer.

This portable first stage codes flat intra blocks and exact quantized luma/chroma residuals at QP 0, integer-pixel motion copies, and P-skip. A residual is used only when local inverse reconstruction matches all input samples and its code is smaller than I_PCM. Other changes use I_PCM. It is useful for scrolling and interoperability, but uncompressed remaining blocks are not suitable for arbitrary high-motion 1080p60 recording. The result borrows the encoder's packet storage until the next call to encode. The AVC configuration remains valid for the encoder's lifetime and uses four-byte NAL lengths.
